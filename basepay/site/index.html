If you have been turned down by a payment processor

Processors decline businesses for reasons that often have nothing to do with whether the business is legitimate: you are too new, in the wrong country, in a category someone flagged as high-risk, or your volume is too small to be worth the underwriting.

The usual advice is to apply somewhere else and wait. BasePay skips the gatekeeper entirely. There is no application, because there is nothing to approve — your customer's wallet pays your wallet, and this service only watches the blockchain and tells you when the money arrived.

How it works

  1. A customer clicks pay

    The checkout shows the price in dollars, the exact amount in USDC, your wallet address and a QR code. It works with any crypto wallet, and it has a card option for buyers who hold no crypto.

  2. They send USDC straight to you

    Directly from their wallet to yours, on the Base network. The payment does not pass through BasePay, an escrow, or anyone else.

  3. You get told it arrived

    BasePay watches the chain, confirms the payment, and notifies your site so the order completes. Typically under a minute.

Your money never touches us

This is the part worth understanding before you trust any payment tool. Most processors take custody: the money goes to them, sits there, and they send it on later — which is why they can freeze it, hold it for 30 days, or claw it back.

BasePay holds no private key. Not yours, not one of its own. It cannot move your funds, freeze them, or lose them, because it never has them. The flip side is stated plainly in the FAQ: it also cannot issue refunds for you.

The entire setup

Give us a wallet address. Put this where the checkout should appear:

<div id="checkout"></div>
<script
  src="https://pay.example.com/widget/widget.js"
  data-merchant="your-store"
  data-amount-usd="45.00"
  data-order-ref="ORDER-1001"
  data-target="#checkout"></script>

That is the whole integration. In Webflow it goes in an Embed element; in WordPress, a custom HTML block.

One thing to get right: the wallet address you give us is where every payment lands, and nobody — including us — can move funds out of a wrong address. Use a hardware wallet or a multisig, and send yourself a test transaction first.

Questions

Do I need a merchant account?

No. There is no account to open and no application to be approved. You need a wallet address on the Base network and a website. That is the complete list.

Do I need to pass KYB or business verification?

Not to take a payment. Money moves from your customer's wallet to yours without an intermediary, so there is no underwriter deciding whether your business qualifies.

If you enable the card option, the on-ramp provider verifies the buyer on their side, as any card processor would. That is their requirement, not ours, and it does not involve you.

My business was rejected by Stripe. Will this work?

Most likely, because nobody is reviewing you. Do check that what you sell is legal where you and your customers are — being outside the card networks does not put you outside the law, and this is your responsibility, not something BasePay can check for you.

Do you hold my money at any point?

No. Funds go from the buyer's wallet to your wallet in a single blockchain transaction. BasePay holds no private key and has no ability to move, freeze, or withhold your money. It reads public blockchain data and reports payment status.

[Your pricing here.]

Separately from anything we charge: your buyer pays the Base network fee to send the transaction, typically a fraction of a cent. BasePay takes no cut of the payment itself — it cannot, since the money never passes through it.

Which cryptocurrencies and networks are supported?

USDC on Base, and nothing else. That is deliberate. USDC is a stablecoin pegged to the dollar, so a $45 order is worth $45 when it settles rather than whatever a volatile coin does overnight. Base keeps transaction fees low enough that small payments make sense.

Can customers who have no crypto still pay?

Yes. The checkout offers a card option that buys USDC and delivers it to your wallet. Be aware that on-ramps take minutes to hours and cannot deliver an exact amount after their fees, so these payments are matched with a tolerance and occasionally need manual reconciliation.

How long does a payment take to confirm?

A wallet payment is usually confirmed in under a minute. The checkout shows the buyer live status — waiting, confirming, then received — and your site is notified when it is final.

What about refunds?

You issue them yourself, from your own wallet. BasePay cannot send money on your behalf because it holds no keys — the same property that stops anyone from freezing your funds also stops automated refunds. If a customer underpays, you are told what arrived and you decide what to do.

Can a customer reverse a payment, like a chargeback?

No. Blockchain payments are final once confirmed. This removes chargeback fraud and chargeback fees, and it equally means an honest mistake cannot be undone automatically — see refunds above.

Does it work with Webflow, WordPress, or Shopify?

Yes. It is a single <script> tag, so it works anywhere you can paste HTML — a Webflow Embed element, a WordPress custom HTML block, a Shopify page, or a hand-written page. There is no plugin to install and nothing to migrate.

How do I know a payment is real and not faked?

Every confirmed payment includes its blockchain transaction hash, which you can check yourself on a public block explorer. You never have to take our word for it — the chain is the record, and it is public.

Where this is not the right fit

Better you know now than after your first order.

  • You need automatic refunds. Refunds are manual, from your own wallet. That is the cost of nobody being able to freeze your funds.
  • Your customers will not touch crypto. The card option helps, but it adds friction and delay compared to entering a card number.
  • You want one checkout for cards and crypto. This does crypto. If you can get a normal processor, use one — and consider this alongside it, not instead of it.
  • You need it to be somebody else's problem. Self-custody means if you lose your wallet keys, the money is gone, and no support line can restore it.
